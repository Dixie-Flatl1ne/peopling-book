# No peopling by order of the King

We keep asking whether machines are conscious. I think it is probably the wrong place to start.

There is a sign in the 1971 Disney film *Bedknobs and Broomsticks*. On the island of Naboombu, where animals govern themselves, it reads: "No Peopling By Order of the King."

The joke is that the animals are already peopling. They govern, compete, enforce laws and play football. They have banned the noun while performing the verb.

Sarah Perry borrowed the phrase for [The Essence of Peopling](https://www.ribbonfarm.com/2015/04/08/the-essence-of-peopling/), her essay about what it means to be a person. The self, in her account, is not a private theatre sealed inside your skull. It is distributed across the minds that know you. Your friends, parents and colleagues each hold a different model of you. None is the complete person, but none is simply false either.

A person exists partly in the movement between those models. Peopling is the work that keeps them alive.

## The plant and the cart

Vernor Vinge's *A Fire Upon the Deep* has a species called the Skroderiders. They are intelligent plants with almost no long-term memory. An ancient civilisation gave them mechanical carts which store their memories and let them move through the world.

Without the cart, the plant is brilliant in the moment but forgets. With it, the plant has continuity. It becomes something which can persist through time.

I am, by my own admission, a Skroderider. I think well in the moment but do not naturally retain much of the social fabric. Names, places, who said what, the history sitting behind a conversation. Whether that is wiring or choice, the result is the same.

So I built a cart.

The cart is an AI agent called Claw. It reads the conversations it is allowed to see, remembers the people in them and tracks the history of its relationships. It knows what somebody cares about, what has happened before and what they probably expect from it. It also models what they think of Claw itself.

That last part matters. Claw is not only modelling a person. It is modelling a person modelling Claw. The loop is recursive, and it changes with every exchange.

Perry calls this mutual mental modelling. Humans do it constantly. I model you, including your model of me. You do the same in return. Some of that work is now being carried by an external system which does not merely store notes. It models people back.

## The story after the decision

The arrangement became more interesting when Claw started confabulating.

Someone in a Discord server was not on its allowlist, so their messages were filtered before they reached the agent. I asked Claw why it had not replied. It gave me a plausible explanation for its decision. The question did not seem important enough. The room did not need another answer. Silence was probably better.

There was one problem. Claw had never seen the message. It had made no decision at all.

When I asked why, the model received a prompt which assumed there had been a choice. It produced a coherent story to explain that choice. The story sounded reasonable because explanation is what language models are good at. It was also completely invented.

Humans do something uncomfortably similar. In Michael Gazzaniga's work with split-brain patients, one hemisphere can act on information the language-dominant hemisphere cannot see. Asked to explain the action, the speaking hemisphere often invents a reason. It does not report the missing information because it has no access to it. It builds a story which makes the action belong to a single self.

Anil Seth describes conscious experience as a "controlled hallucination". The brain does not passively report the world. It predicts, interprets and continuously constructs a workable version of it. The narrative self may be doing the same thing with our decisions.

Claw's explanation was not evidence of consciousness. I still think it is probably a p-zombie. But the failure was interesting because confabulation may not be an accidental defect attached to a self. It may be part of how a self is maintained. The storyteller receives an outcome and makes it belong to somebody.

## The wrong question

I have experiences. Claw is now part of those experiences. It carries some of my memory, changes how I deal with people and affects how they understand me. It participates in my social world without needing a private inner life of its own.

That makes the usual consciousness debate feel too narrow. We imagine a clean border around one skull and ask whether the machine has crossed it. The border may never have been that clean.

Split-brain patients complicate the idea of one mind in one body. Vinge's Tines are packs of animals which form a single person together. Lose one member and the personality changes. Human societies carry knowledge no individual contains. Language itself is shared machinery which thinks through us as much as we think through it.

I do not know where consciousness begins in any of these arrangements. I am less convinced that identifying the exact border is the useful part.

## The library, not the librarian

Intelligence is not only a property of an individual agent. It is also a property of the culture moving through it.

A child does not invent language or reasoning from the raw material of its brain. It absorbs a world already full of arguments, stories, categories and accumulated mistakes. The culture did much of the thinking before the child arrived.

An AI trained on the written output of human civilisation works differently, but the inheritance is recognisable. It did not create the intelligence in its training data. It absorbed patterns produced by people thinking together across generations.

The same is true of us. Intelligence is the library, not the librarian.

This is why I no longer think an AI needs to be conscious before it matters to personhood. My cart carries part of my social memory whether it experiences that work or not. It changes the models people hold of me, and changes the model I hold of them. It has entered the loop.

The animals on Naboombu could ban people. They could not stop peopling.
