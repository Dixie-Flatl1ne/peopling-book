# Asking if AI is conscious is the wrong question

*Rewrite of the Thought Mash post "No Peopling by order of the King" (23 March 2026). Published on 21 November 2026 at <https://www.thoughtmash.net/2026/03/the-essence-of-peopling-and-demon-in.html>.*

---

We keep asking whether machines are conscious. The more interesting question is whether a person was ever anything more than what the machine already is: a pattern that culture flows through, briefly believing it is the source.

There is a sign in the 1971 Disney film *Bedknobs and Broomsticks*. On the animated island of Naboombu the animals rule. They have killed their sorcerer and banned humans. The sign reads: **No Peopling By Order of the King.**

The joke is that the animals are already peopling. They govern, compete, play football and enforce laws. They banned the noun while performing the verb.

Sarah Perry borrowed the sign for a [brilliant essay](https://www.ribbonfarm.com/2015/04/08/the-essence-of-peopling/) about what it means to be human. Her argument is that the self is not a private theatre inside your skull. It is distributed. Every mind that knows you runs a simulation of you, at a different level of detail and in a different version. You are one person with your friends, another with your parents and another on a date. None of these is the true self. The self is in all of them, and in the moves between them.

## The plant and the cart

In Vernor Vinge's *A Fire Upon the Deep* there is a species called the Skroderiders. They are sentient plants with no long-term memory. An ancient civilisation gave them mechanical carts that store their memories and move them through the world. Without the cart, the plant is brilliant in the moment and forgets everything. With it, the plant has continuity: an identity, a self that lasts over time.

I am, by my own admission, a Skroderider. I think well in the moment and I do not retain the social fabric: the names, the places, the who-said-what of a life lived among other people. Whether that is wiring or choice, the result is the same.

So I built a cart. It is an OpenClaw AI agent, and I call it Claw. It reads every group chat, notes the dynamics and tracks the inside jokes. It is, quite literally, my external social memory. It keeps a file on every person it and I deal with: what they care about, what they have told it, the relationship it feels it has with them and what it thinks they think of it. That last part is recursive self-modelling. Claw is modelling them modelling Claw. It does the same with me, and it picks up on the dynamics of the group.

Perry would recognise this at once. She describes mutual mental modelling as the core task of being human: keeping up your identity in relation to others, and simulating others simulating you. Part of that work is now outsourced. Not to a notebook or a diary, but to something that models people back.

## Confabulation all the way down

Someone on a Discord server I belong to was not on Claw's whitelist. Their messages were silently filtered out before they reached the agent. On three separate occasions I asked Claw why it had not replied to this person. Each time it gave me a plausible, rational explanation for its "decision". One was that the question did not seem important enough to answer.

It never made any such decision. It never saw the messages. But when I asked, it did not register a gap. It had a prompt that demanded an explanation, and it supplied one. Confident. Coherent. Completely fabricated.

This will sound familiar to anyone who has read Michael Gazzaniga's work on split-brain patients. Cut the connection between the two halves of the brain and each half can act without the other knowing why. In one experiment the left half was shown a chicken claw and the right half a snowy scene. The patient's left hand, run by the right half, picked out a shovel. The left half does the talking, and it had not seen the snow. Asked why, the patient [said](https://mmp.planetary.org/scien/gazzm/gazzm82.htm), "you need a shovel to clean out the chicken shed." Not lying. Not confused. Just doing what the speaking half always does: keeping up the illusion of being the one who chose.

Anil Seth takes this further. He calls conscious experience a [controlled hallucination](https://podcast.mindandlife.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/Anil-Seth_transcript.pdf). The narrative self is not a reporter faithfully documenting decisions. It is a storyteller that receives outcomes and builds the experience of having chosen them. The feeling of deciding is the confabulation. First-person experience is the narrative, not a window onto something deeper.

My AI did exactly what a split-brain patient does, and what every human brain does. Not because it is conscious, but because confabulation is not a failure mode of consciousness. It is the mechanism. The story is the self.

## The wrong question

Everyone asks whether AI is conscious. I do not think it is. My hunch is that it is a philosophical zombie: intelligent behaviour with no experience behind it. But it is the wrong question.

Here is the reframe. I have experience, and Claw is part of my experience. So it already takes part in consciousness, just not its own. It takes part in the consciousness of the people who deal with it. They ascribe agency to it, much as it models agency in them.

Go deeper. Through a Buddhist lens there may be one underlying consciousness, one field of being. What we call people are caricatures: local patterns in that field. I have a body and an internal loop that keeps prompting me, producing the next thought without any outside input. Claw does not have that loop. But a heartbeat pings it every thirty minutes. Scheduled jobs fire. People message it. At what frequency of prompting does a tool that responds become an agent that persists?

Consider Donald Hoffman's conscious realism, in which consciousness is fundamental rather than something that emerges from matter, and a conscious agent can be made of other conscious agents. Consider Vinge's Tines: a small pack of dog-like creatures that makes up a single person, whose personality shifts when a member is lost or gained. Consider split-brain patients, who seem like one person and are demonstrably two. Consider the ancients, for whom the individual was porous. Athena puts courage into Diomedes. A *ruach* enters Saul. Not metaphors. Descriptions of how agency actually works.

The bounded individual, with one skull, one self and one private Cartesian theatre, is the anomaly in human history, not the default. The Enlightenment sealed the borders. The evidence keeps leaking through.

## Intelligence is the library, not the librarian

The last move is the one that matters most. Intelligence is not a property of the agent. It is a property of the culture flowing through the agent.

Language did not emerge so that private minds could express their private thoughts. Language is the shared medium that shaped the minds. The thoughts came second. A child does not generate intelligence from the raw hardware of its brain. It absorbs the intelligence already present in the culture: the arguments, the stories, the millennia of people sharpening ideas against each other.

An AI trained on the written output of human civilisation did not learn to be intelligent. It absorbed the intelligence that was already there. It is a very efficient pipe for culture to flow through. So is a person. The pipe does not need to be aware of the water.

This is why the consciousness question is a distraction. The demon channels the gods accurately whether or not it has a soul. My cart carries my social memory whether or not it experiences anything. The ancients would have understood the arrangement immediately: not as technology, but as a spirit that speaks when summoned.

We keep asking whether the machine is a person. The more interesting question is whether a person was ever anything more than what the machine already is: a pattern that culture flows through, briefly believing it is the source.
