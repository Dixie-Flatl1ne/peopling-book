Like most things, it had much more to do with politics than religious ideals.

If I were to blame one person, it would be **Constantine the Great**, as he organised the following:

- The [Council of Nicaea](https://www.britannica.com/event/First-Council-of-Nicaea-325). I would argue that vilifying [Arianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arianism#:~:text=Arianism%20is%20a%20nontrinitarian%20Christological,eternal%20with%20God%20the%20Father.) in favour of Trinitarianism created a fundamental religious split over whether Jesus is God.
- The founding of Constantinople, which led to a Greek vs. Latin political divide and eventually to the [East-West Schism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East%E2%80%93West_Schism).

Constantine made some bad calls.

## Christianity-Islam split

The reason for the Council was that Roman Emperor Constantine wanted to enforce one religious doctrine across the Empire in order to ideologically unify its citizens. The main ideological issue is the nature of Jesus, with Christians arguing Jesus is God (as part of the Trinity), whereas Muslims (and Christian Arians) argue Jesus was just a prophet.

Islam views itself as a purer form of the same monotheistic faith. Islam's main issue with Christianity is Christianity's insistence on the divine nature of Jesus. Arguably, Muhammad's teachings and military might may have made Islam irreconcilable with Christianity anyway (like Joseph Smith and Mormonism), but it's hard to know for sure. It's also hard to know where Muhammad picked up his ideas. [See here](http://orthodoxinfo.com/general/stjohn_islam.aspx): *For example, John of Damascus (d. 749), a Christian writer, states that Muhammad "having chanced upon the Old and New Testaments and likewise, it seems, having conversed with an Arian monk, devised his own heresy."*

Arabs conquer half of the Roman Empire.

At the time of the Arab conquests, Christianity remained united, with the Patriarch of Constantinople being the dominant leader, not the Pope in Rome.

## Sunni and Shi'i split in 656 AD: Sunni split from Shiite

This originated from Arab infighting over who was the rightful successor to Muhammad. The Sunnis beat the Shiites at the Battle of the Camel, which is why most of the Muslim world is Sunni.

![](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEizbTKRFjeEXmnuoBcsBQzHBrBdoIjkI7B3If88JXLzW49R_J_HhenVt4TUK3AaPl6THKq2EL-fAnTnhqK5hOOMI1jfcCvylWoULMGMJ9O-Wvt2z6UNSEVR_Om1jw_H2MCw3y5HwYBPjcAL/s1600/wa_img_karbala_interactive_map.jpg)

Arabs get into a fight over who's boss of the new Empire. Sunnis win. Persians hijack the Shiite cause to retain their territorial and political independence.

The Persian Empire began forcibly converting its land to Shia Islam, starting with [Ismail I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ismail_I), who began the Safavid dynasty and wanted an additional instrument for preventing the Ottoman Empire from easily conquering and assimilating its territory. So this ideological split was introduced to unite peoples within the Persian Empire against the rest of the Muslim world.

## Christianity split into Roman Catholic and Eastern Orthodox

Constantine split the running of the Empire, with the Greek-run portions managed separately from the Latin-run portions. This had happened before, but he also set up a second centre of power to rival Rome: Constantinople. Rivalry between the Pope in Rome and the Patriarch in Constantinople led to the East-West Schism. The Eastern Empire became Eastern Orthodox (and spread this to the Slavs in Kievan Rus), while the successor states of the Western Roman Empire, notably the Carolingian Empire and its successors, France and the Holy Roman Empire, became Catholic.

Constantine splits the Roman Empire into East and West. This went on to become the Roman Catholic-Eastern Orthodox split. He also enforced the doctrine of the Trinity and outlawed Arianism.

The East-West Schism leads to the Roman Catholic-Eastern Orthodox divide. Basically, it boils down to the Pope of Rome and the Patriarch of Constantinople getting into a pissing contest.

## Roman Catholic-Protestant split

After the Romans lost the [Battle of the Teutoburg Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Teutoburg_Forest) against German tribes, Rome never again tried to conquer and assimilate the German people. Protestantism spread almost exclusively among Germanic successor states (where Germanic languages are spoken).

![](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEgm0NsNMCqVJ1EW4qrNuRTeIpFk1eEdQA7XZoe-gRg8VLa_8lAwbN-EtAHRon9ZM-3wHJPLc3VCbWXZaly1BbQLsakkeOW4BtTXu2ieEF2yTMu-ShWqFUEF8zVVLCOLPW4ThauOT-w3Rg0n/s1600/germanic-romance-slavic-language-groups.png)

Language map: there is a complete overlap between Protestants and Germanic-speaking peoples. The only Germanic-speaking people that remained Catholic are those who were part of the Habsburg Empire.

As these regions never spoke a Romance language, they felt especially disconnected from a church whose religious ceremonies were carried out in Latin. Once the Bible was translated well by Martin Luther into German and disseminated by the printing press, people began to question the 'corruptions' in contemporary Roman Catholic teaching and challenge papal authority.

This could have led to reform of the Catholic Church without a split from the Church, so why did Germans, Scandinavians and the English split from the Catholic Church? Because the rulers of these regions wanted more power over the Church, and the Pope's main enforcer, the Holy Roman Emperor, no longer had the power to enforce unity.

This lack of power was ironically due to the Pope's own policies against the Holy Roman Emperor in previous centuries. The Pope supported the Northern Italians and German barons against the Emperor, leading to defeat in Northern Italy and appeasement in Germany. In time, this led to further decentralisation of the Empire, which culminated in a religious 'civil war', the [Thirty Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty_Years%27_War), that destroyed Germany and German religious unity. The Holy Roman Emperor could not enforce Catholicism on his subjects. The Empire's princes chose their own flavours of Protestantism (Luther, Calvin, etc.).

## So why blame Constantine?

Putting it simply, Constantine shouldn't have just deferred decision-making at the Council of Nicaea to bishops and shouldn't have accepted such a divisive outcome. He was the ultimate decision-maker and had been using Christianity for political gain. It happens all the time with politicians who don't necessarily care too much about what the ideology they're using for their own success is actually about... For example, Trump said the Bible is his favourite book, but when asked what his favourite verse is, he just said, "I like all of it." Now, the irony that Constantine didn't care about or believe in the religion he contributed to institutionalising isn't lost on me, but it's just life.

Assuming that the critique St. John of Damascus made of Islam is true, and that the resolution to the Arian controversy had been more moderate, either Islam would never have arisen (arguably it would have anyway, given, say, Joseph Smith), or it would have arisen as a more reconcilable "heresy". Islam was originally classified as a heresy, not a separate religion.

If that assumption holds true, then the Church ends up being more flexible around doctrine. Muhammad's heresy could fit in the envelope, and the East-West Schism would have been less likely, which is likely to have affected the Crusades, especially the Fourth. Therefore, even if the Saracens (how Arab desert dwellers were originally identified, not by their religion but by where they came from) had conquered large swathes or all of the Eastern Empire, they would likely have learned Greek and been assimilated, much like the Mongols were in China, into the Christian 'Greek' religion. Let's face it, the New Testament was written in Greek. It would have been far more compromising and therefore more unifying.

Now, specifically in Asia Minor, this does assume that inland Anatolians would not have been assimilated if a Christian religion were more tolerant of heresy, while a counterpoint could be made that inland Anatolians were assimilated by the Turks because they had more in common with nomadic Turks than coastal-dwelling Greeks.

Why is this Constantine's fault? Well, while he had the 'divinely inspired' dream about Christianity, used it as a rallying call and won a civil war, he didn't even necessarily believe in it, and if he did, maybe he was an Arianist. So why the big push for Trinitarianism? The reason is that he didn't care. He just wanted a unifying ideology, and he deferred complete authority over the proceedings to bishops who excommunicated and exiled Arius. But his 'heresy' lived on for centuries, arguably 'inspiring' (if not divinely inspiring) Islam. If he had taken a more active role in the proceedings, he would no doubt have elected for a more pragmatic outcome. He didn't see what the big deal was. He just wanted a unifying force for the Empire that wasn't emperor worship, which had been the way things worked previously.

St. John's view: "From that time to the present a false prophet named Mohammed has appeared in their midst. This man, after having chanced upon the Old and New Testaments and likewise, it seems, having conversed with an Arian monk, devised his own heresy." This is a statement I believe because I don't believe in God or divine inspiration. I don't think angels visited Muhammad, or that a gospel written in Greek in about 70 AD about an Aramaic guy who died in about 30 AD is factually correct. I do, however, understand how a religion can act as a catalyst for cultural assimilation. Look at Latin America, Western Europe, or the Arab world if you need examples.
