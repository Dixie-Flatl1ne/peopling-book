So according to these [Citigroup memos](http://politicalgates.blogspot.com/2011/12/citigroup-plutonomy-memos-two-bombshell.html), there is a [managerial technocratic aristocracy](http://1v99.blogspot.com/2011/12/citigroup-new-managerial-aristocracy.html) in the [Anglosphere](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglosphere) paying itself massive salaries and making zillions from start-ups. The Anglosphere countries function as [plutonomies](http://www.investopedia.com/terms/p/plutonomy.asp#axzz1iB4sK2Fn), while other Western nations are more egalitarian.

> Characterizing the U.S. Plutonomy: Based on the Consumer Finance Survey, the Top 1% Accounted For 20% of Income, 40% of Financial Wealth and 33% of Net Worth in the U.S. (More Than the Net Worth of the Bottom 95% Households Put Together) in 2001

Now, fundamentally, as a species, we are egalitarian by nature. Is what is happening unnatural or even immoral, or is it just the [Pareto principle](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pareto_principle) playing out, along with suitable financial incentives for business leaders and technological innovators? I don't know myself. However, I do think most individuals don't need millions or billions of dollars as motivation to be productive.

Having said that, having a lot of wealth in the hands of a few may seem unfair, but I would argue only if it adversely affects the human condition of the 99%. Keep in mind, 99% of tennis Grand Slams are won by 1% of tennis players, 99% of blog entries are produced by 1% of bloggers (I'm making the numbers up), etc., etc. My point is that it is natural to have an elite dominating any given domain due to specialisation.

This isn't something trivial like tennis, however. Our wealth has a large impact on our quality of life. We in the West aren't that badly off. Sure, you could have more toys, but I would argue that as long as we retain open access to the internet and information goods, the quality of human experience will remain comparable across the board. As information goods and services consumption continues to grow as a proportion of GDP, as long as we all have open access to a large chunk of them, inequality won't be as bad as the numbers look. Yes, there will be material-goods inequality, but not huge inequalities in terms of human experience or quality of life, which will shift more and more to the consumption of information, bypassing tangible goods. Given that the marginal cost of propagating information goods and services for consumption is near zero, in the long run, this material imbalance will cease to matter as much, provided patents and intellectual property law are reformed.

The real worry, then, is if these Anglo plutonomies manage to block access to information goods and services in order to further enrich the financial elite. The system is trying to clamp down on intellectual property and internet access ([SOPA](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stop_Online_Piracy_Act)), but even if it does, the capital-creation fluctuations introduced by newly patented technology will grow to a point where the markets will lose their ability to accurately measure value and capital will become meaningless. If Google can create 200 billion of market cap mainly down to a patented [PageRank](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PageRank) algorithm, then things are going to get out of control when some real information goods and services start appearing. So this may be the ‘last gasp’ of the financial elite.
