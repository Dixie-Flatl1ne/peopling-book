# Lucid Dreaming: reality construct flaws & useful reality check cues

Post ID: 8990026033186183595

Published: 2010-12-17T13:02:00+11:00

A reality test is a method of determining if you are awake or dreaming by noticing differences between the dream world and the real world. Here is a brief guide to some reality tests I perform.

Dreams are generally pretty different from real life, and lots of strange things happen, yet most of the time you won't notice. The main reason you don't notice is that your consciousness doesn't seem to operate too many critical-thinking processes while you dream, making you very unlikely to notice something unrealistic that is occurring. You might think something is a bit odd, but you will either explain it away or not even think about it.

In the majority of my dreams, I never become lucid, but here are some reality checks I perform once I suspect that my current stream of consciousness might be processing ‘dream’ as opposed to ‘real’ stimuli.

**General:**

Wrong things that sometimes occur. You can't do these things in every dream. Some dreams seem to be more ‘real’ than others. Generally, you can do a bunch of these or none at all:

- I can fly.
- I can will things to move.
- I can walk or float through walls or ceilings.
- I can flick a light switch and the light level doesn't change. Sometimes the light level does change. [*Waking Life*](http://www.imdb.com/title/tt0243017/) got this wrong.
- I can will things to disappear.
- I can will things to appear.
- I am walking or driving in a familiar area, but roads or street signs are wrong or missing.

**Mirrors:**

My dream construct has big problems generating proper-looking reflections in mirrors. If I ‘glance’ at a mirror for an instant, it will probably look fine, but if I start moving around, things start getting interesting:

- My image becomes distorted and does not look like me.
- My image isn't ‘mirroring’ me properly if I wave my hand, etc., although it generally does a good job of this.
- My image distorts and takes on a life of its own. Suddenly, it is another person.

**Gravity:**

The dream construct doesn't seem to have any idea that when you fall, you should accelerate (I guess it's kinda counting on your inbuilt instinct not to jump off high things). When falling or even descending stairs, things are sluggish. Falling is just floating at a moderate pace in a downward direction. If you try to run down stairs quickly, you will often start floating slowly in a forward and downward motion.

**People:**

Dream characters are a rather simple bunch. Crowds seem to act as a single entity, moving as a mob and exhibiting the same kinds of behaviour. Most people seem unable to interact with each other or me. Generally, there are one to three ‘interactive’ people who will change their behaviour based on what you do and talk to you. They generally can't interact with each other or even notice each other. Occasionally, when the dream construct stuffs up, you will get two people talking to you at the same time, oblivious to the fact that the other is talking. The majority of other people in a crowd won't respond to you talking to them, etc., unless you grab them. Then some kind of behavioural pattern will kick in, which could involve them speaking gobbledygook or exhibiting a behaviour, for example, hostility, happiness or even flirting with you, but they won't hold a conversation.

People seem to be assigned roles that, under normal circumstances, you would never notice. If a person is programmed to just walk down a street past you and can't do much else, unless you realise you are dreaming, you are unlikely to notice, as you wouldn't normally approach a random person on the street and start trying to talk to them. I would say the AI in many modern computer games equals or exceeds my dream construct. A crowd can interact with you as a crowd, for example, chase you, run away from you or, most likely, ignore you.

**Fight-or-flight dream:**

Having said all that, you could be able to do everything above and still not realise you're dreaming. Exactly how the critical-reasoning processes get kicked off is still a mystery to me, but fight-or-flight dreams trigger them regularly.

Fight or flight is one of my common dream scenarios, common to many people, probably some kind of dream-training programme, like a fire alarm drill. Either I am pursuing someone or being pursued, generally either on foot or in a car. Whenever some kind of monster or bad guy shows up, I seem to immediately realise I'm dreaming. I haven't had a nightmare since I was a kid because whenever dreams are supposed to get scary, I either realise it's a dream or I fly above them or will them to disappear, etc., without realising I am dreaming. A common situation is chasing or being chased up and down corridors. If I am chasing, gravity not working when I'm descending stairs generally gives the game away. If I am being chased, quite interestingly, bad guys often don't come within one to two metres of me even if I am motionless. They just kind of sit there looking menacing until I start moving again.

**Trade-off between dream quality and lucidity:**

I have noticed that the more lucid I become, the harder it is to maintain the dream. Perhaps there isn't enough brain-processing power to go around. Either your higher mind creates your environment, or it thinks rationally about things. I find I have to ‘ride the wave’ and try to stick to the ‘dream script’ in order to maintain the dream.
