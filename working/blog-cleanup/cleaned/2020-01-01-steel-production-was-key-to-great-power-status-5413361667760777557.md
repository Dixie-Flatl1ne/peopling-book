In recent history steel was the most important thing for winning wars. Notice how the top four producers of steel from 200 years ago are permanent members of the UN Security Council?

The missing permanent member is China, which now produces quite a lot:

![Country comparison steel production](https://wolfstreet.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/06/World-steel-production-largest-by-country-2018.png)

However, these days steel isn't the most important thing for winning wars. Information technology for intelligence gathering, disinformation, smart weapons command and control and many other things is critical. This is why a lot of the China-US trade war revolves around the U.S. trying to prevent too many information technology transfers (as well as protecting strategic U.S. steel & aluminium industries from Chinese undercutting).

Steel still matters, but the commanding heights have moved. The state which controls computation, networks and advanced weapons may have the same advantage industrial steel production once provided.
