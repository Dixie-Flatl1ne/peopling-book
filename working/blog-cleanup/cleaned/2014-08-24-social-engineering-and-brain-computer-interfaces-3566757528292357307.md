One of the discussions that I feel is important for us to have is how we will overcome the 'social inertia' around a set hierarchy of organisms in this brave new world we are entering.

My own personal pet causes are [David Pearce](https://www.facebook.com/davidpearce)'s abolition of suffering and ending factory farming, but I differ on how I would implement it. David argues we should all become vegetarians and therefore end the need for slaughterhouses. But this also ends the need for a large percentage of our farm animals. Pigs, if given a choice, may prefer the butcher's knife to non-existence.

What I feel is important is developing brain-computer interfaces (BCIs) not only for ourselves, but also for our domestic animals. There is a large untapped reservoir of thought capacity within our cows, pigs and sheep which we should utilise. Not only that, but it would make our domestic animals too valuable as knowledge workers for us to slaughter them!

The next step (unless you are a die-hard Kurzweilian) is genetic engineering in conjunction with BCIs, with weak AI supporting subsystems. Things get rather hazy after this point. The binding problem comes into play, etc... I would rather focus on the near term, as we can use our shared morals and ethics (which we, as members of Western society, share 99% of, let's face it) to evaluate an incremental change.

One proposed change which has received a heated response is the gender ratio that is best for society. We have been manipulating the gender ratios of our domesticated animals for millennia. For instance, most bulls are killed as they don't produce any milk. So I think it is fair to evaluate what is best for human society.

There are many science fiction stories which do a good job of presenting an all-female or female-dominated society. One of my favourites is 'Houston, Houston, Do You Read?' by James Tiptree Jr., which is the pen name of Alice Hastings Bradley. She didn't want the publicity of being a female sci-fi writer and so chose a male pseudonym to write about an all-female future society (no, the irony isn't lost on me).

Anyway... getting to the point... Yes, I have been waffling on for quite some time... this is going somewhere, people! In the book, the astronauts learn that all men have died out and their rescue party is from an all-female society which has no intention of reintroducing men, as that could challenge the current ruling elite. The men are to be studied and eliminated.

I would argue that it isn't so much about which society is right or wrong, but rather how you can change society without facing severe 'blowback' from the incumbent elite who stand to lose from the change you wish to implement. This applies regardless of whether you want to increase human potential through genetic engineering (eugenics), increase the participation of biomass in society (BCIs for animals), or even remove men from society. I would argue that once artificial wombs come online, the all-female argument is diluted substantially. How can we make the big changes without the mass violence that normally comes hand in hand with them? Think of the social revolution that occurred in tandem with industrialisation.
